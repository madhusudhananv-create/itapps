3
Step 3
Establish What Caused the Problem (Root Cause)

There are a number of tried and tested techniques available to establish root cause; training should be given to the people in an organisation who would have this responsibility — e.g. 5 Why’s. It is often far too easy to just state “human error.”

So What Are The 5 Why’s?
5 Whys is an iterative interrogative technique used to explore the cause-and-effect relationships underlying a particular problem. The primary goal is to determine the root cause of a defect or problem by repeating the question “Why?” — each answer forming the basis of the next question.

The “5” derives from an anecdotal observation on the average number of iterations needed to resolve the problem.
The 5 Whys interrogative chain
3-legged 5 Whys approach

Example: CNC Machine That Stopped Mid Operation

Why?
"Why did the machine stop?" — The circuit has overloaded, causing a fuse to blow.
Why?
"Why is the circuit overloaded?" — There wasn’t enough lubrication on the bearings, so they locked up.
Why?
"Why was there not enough lubrication on the bearings?" — The oil pump on the machine was not circulating sufficient oil.
Why?
"Why is the pump not circulating sufficient oil?" — The pump intake was clogged with metal shavings.
Why?
"Why is the intake clogged with metal shavings?" — Because there was no filter on the pump. Root Cause
Example A — Employee & Hole
Why?
Why did the employee fall down the hole? — The hole had no barrier & no communication to staff that such works were taking place.
Why?
Why were there no barrier & no communication? — The contractor entered the premises unannounced & commenced works without making their presence known.
Why?
Why did they not report to reception / make their presence known? — No one informed the contractor when to arrive & of the processes on site.
Why?
Why was the contractor not informed? — No system in place or Permit to Work for managing contractors on site. Root Cause
Example B — Wrong Item
Why?
Why was the wrong item ordered & sent? — Wrong details entered into system by a new member of staff unfamiliar with the company’s products.
Why?
Why was it possible to enter the wrong details? — System does not prompt to check & confirm details with the customer & allows any member of staff to take orders. Root Cause
4
Step 4
Determine & Implement Action to Prevent Re-Occurrence (Corrective Action)
Example A
Implement a Permit to Work System, purchase site signage to instruct all contractors to enter via reception, and communicate these arrangements to contractors before they come on site.
Example B
Add a check into the system that prompts checking with the customer what they ordered. Add a log-in to the system with competence requirements required to be given a log-in.
5
Step 5
Review the Effectiveness of the Corrective Action Taken ‐ Did It Work?

This is essentially a small audit of the above proposed corrective actions — firstly checking that they were actually carried out, then looking for examples of similar activities that have taken place since, and verifying that the initial problem did not re-occur. This step can be justifiably left over for several months to allow sufficient time to pass. It’s to check that the corrective actions have been embedded properly.

Example A
Check that a Permit to Work System has been implemented and suitable site signage purchased and fitted. Then look for work that has been done on site, checking that the arrangements had been communicated to the contractors before they came on site, that they signed in at reception, and were not permitted to carry out works until they had been inducted and a permit to work issued.
Example B
Check that the new step has been added into the system that prompts checking with the customer what they ordered. Check that a log-in to the system has been added with competence requirements required for such. Then look at the newest employees to ensure that they have the correct competence for any log-in that they may have been given.
